Why do these rays divide the page?+

As the direction turns, the supporting line usually touches one vertex. At a boundary direction, it touches an entire edge: the highlighted vertex changes. These edge-normal directions are the critical rays used to divide this page.

The pale regions are separated by parabolic margins around the critical rays. In coordinates along and across a ray, their shape is |t| ≤ c√s: their angular width tends to zero at infinity. Here they give the sections room to breathe; they do not describe a zero set. The small rings open short notes, borrowing the visual idea of zeros near a boundary.

In my work on exponential sums, this geometry helps distinguish which exponential term dominates in each direction. The navigation is an adaptation of Figure 1 and §3.1 of my public preprint. Sections and keywords form an editorial topic map; their positions are illustrative.

For W = {conjugates of the frequencies λⱼ}, let K = conv(W). A direction θ selects a vertex maximizing Re(λⱼ eⁱᶿ). Critical rays are outward normals to the edges of K; on a boundary, the adjacent vertices tie.
